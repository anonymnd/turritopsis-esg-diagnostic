# International ESG Questionnaire Benchmark

This benchmark looks at international sustainability questionnaire systems to improve the Turritopsis ESG application.

## 1. EcoVadis

Source: https://support.ecovadis.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002531507-What-is-the-EcoVadis-methodology

Useful ideas:

- Assessment is customized by activity, size, and location.
- Methodology evaluates management systems through policies, actions, and results.
- Scorecards show strengths and improvement areas.
- Supporting documents matter.
- Quality, traceability, confidentiality, and analyst review are important.

What we should learn:

- Do not only ask "Do you have this practice?"
- Ask whether there is a policy, action, deployment, reporting, and result.
- Show improvement areas clearly.
- Keep proof confidential.
- Add human reviewer validation for serious scores.

## 2. B Impact Assessment

Source: https://kb.bimpactassessment.net/en/support/solutions/articles/43000574683

Useful ideas:

- Impact areas include Governance, Workers, Community, Environment, and Customers.
- It distinguishes operational impact and impact business models.
- The questionnaire is stakeholder-oriented.

What we should learn:

- ESG should not feel abstract.
- Questions should explain who is affected: employees, customers, community, environment, governance bodies.
- Later versions could add customer/community dimensions if needed.

## 3. CDP

Sources:

- https://www.cdp.net/en/disclosure-2026
- https://www.cdp.net/en/disclose/how-to-disclose
- https://www.cdp.net/en/faqs

Useful ideas:

- Questionnaires are sector/theme tailored.
- Guidance and scoring materials are available.
- CDP scoring has levels such as disclosure, awareness, management, and leadership.
- It aligns with global frameworks such as IFRS S2, TCFD, GRI, TNFD, SBTN, GHG Protocol.

What we should learn:

- Add guidance inside each question.
- Make the questionnaire sector-specific.
- Separate "disclosure completeness" from "performance maturity."
- For climate, ask for data quality, emissions, targets, and verification.

## 4. EFRAG VSME

Sources:

- https://www.efrag.org/en/smes-and-sustainability-reporting
- https://knowledgehub.efrag.org/interactive/vsme/vsme-standard-annex-i

Useful ideas:

- VSME is voluntary and designed for non-listed SMEs.
- It helps SMEs respond to data requests from large companies, banks, and investors.
- It has a Basic Module and a Comprehensive Module.
- Basic topics include energy/GHG, pollution, biodiversity, water, circular economy/waste, workforce, health and safety, remuneration/training, and anti-corruption fines.

What we should learn:

- Create two levels in the app:
  - Basic diagnostic
  - Comprehensive diagnostic
- Keep the SME version simple.
- Align outputs with bank/investor data needs.

## 5. IFRS / ISSB

Source: https://www.ifrs.org/sustainability/knowledge-hub/introduction-to-issb-and-ifrs-sustainability-disclosure-standards/

Useful ideas:

- IFRS S1 and S2 use four content areas:
  - governance
  - strategy
  - risk management
  - metrics and targets
- Materiality matters.

What we should learn:

- Our final report should include:
  - governance of ESG
  - strategy and roadmap
  - risks
  - metrics and targets
- AI should flag when a claimed score has no metric or target.

## 6. UN Global Compact CoP

Sources:

- https://unglobalcompact.org/participation/report/cop
- https://unglobalcompact.org/library/6107

Useful ideas:

- Annual questionnaire model.
- Progress communication is standardized.
- Questionnaire guidebooks explain the reason behind each question.

What we should learn:

- Add annual reporting year.
- Allow score updates over time.
- Add guidance explaining why each question matters.

## Better Vision For Turritopsis

The Turritopsis app should combine:

- VSME simplicity for SMEs.
- EcoVadis-style proof and scorecard feedback.
- CDP-style guidance and questionnaire tailoring.
- IFRS-style governance/strategy/risk/metrics language.
- Human validation before calling any score official.
